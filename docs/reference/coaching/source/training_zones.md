# ECHO Running Coach — Training Zones

**Verified against McMillan Running Methodology**

---

## Zone Overview

The ECHO coaching system uses 5 training zones based on heart rate percentage, RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion), and pace feel. All zone targets must be calibrated to the individual runner's max heart rate and current fitness level.

> ⚠️ **GAP — NEEDS IAN:** The zone boundaries in this file disagree with `ECHO_Framework_CORRECTED.md` Pillar 4: here Zone 3 = 80-87% and Zone 4 = 87-95% of max HR; the framework has Zone 3 = 80-90% and Zone 4 = 90-95% (Zone 2's physiology also differs — "marathon pace to slightly faster" here vs "right at lactate threshold boundary" there). A generator emitting HR ranges needs one authoritative set. Which governs?

### Max Heart Rate Estimation

**Formula:** 220 - age (estimate only)

**Example:** Age 35 runner = estimated max HR 185 bpm

**Important:** This is an estimate. A field test (hill sprint or track time trial) gives more accurate individual max HR.

> 📌 **RULING (Ian, 2026-07-10):** **Keep 220 − age.** Ian was shown Tanaka et al. 2001 (meta-analysis: 351 studies, 18,712 subjects), which found 208 − 0.7 × age more accurate and ±10-12 bpm of individual scatter around *any* age formula — and chose to keep 220 − age. Do not "fix" this formula without a new ruling from Ian. (Note the ±10-12 bpm scatter when interpreting any age-derived zone: it is an estimate either way.)

## Zone 1 — Easy / Recovery

**Purpose:** Aerobic base building, active recovery, economy development

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Target % of Max HR | 60-70% |
| Example (max HR 185) | 111-130 bpm |
| RPE | 1-3 out of 10 |
| Breathing | Fully controlled, rhythmic, can sing |
| Conversation | Full sentences, no effort to speak |
| Feel | Very comfortable, almost too easy |
| Pace feel | Significantly slower than race pace |
| Weekly usage | 60-70% of all training volume |
| Duration | 30-60 min easy; 60-120 min long runs |

### ECHO Focus in Zone 1

- **Economy:** This is where economy is built. Keep HR truly low.
- **Cadence:** Target 170-180 SPM. Practice and establish rhythm.
- **Harmony:** Best zone for form work. No fatigue = perfect form practice.
- **Optimization:** Discipline. Resist racing. Stay truly easy.

### Common Mistake

Running Zone 1 sessions at Zone 2 intensity. This is the **#1 error** in recreational runners. It accumulates fatigue without the recovery benefit. Easy must mean EASY.

**Coach Cue:**
> If you feel like you could run faster, you are at the right pace. Stay there. The adaptation happens here.

## Zone 2 — Aerobic Base / Steady State

**Purpose:** Aerobic capacity building, tempo base, fat oxidation

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Target % of Max HR | 70-80% |
| Example (max HR 185) | 130-148 bpm |
| RPE | 4-5 out of 10 |
| Breathing | Deeper and more noticeable, but fully controlled |
| Conversation | Short sentences, slight effort to speak |
| Feel | Comfortably hard, sustainable for 60-90 minutes |
| Pace feel | Marathon pace to slightly faster |
| Weekly usage | 15-20% of training volume |
| Duration | 20-40 minutes sustained, or part of longer run |

### ECHO Focus in Zone 2

- **Economy:** Tests aerobic efficiency at meaningful effort
- **Cadence:** Maintain 175-180 SPM — harder under Zone 2 effort
- **Harmony:** Shoulders stay relaxed despite increased effort
- **Optimization:** Discipline to hold this pace, not push to Zone 3

### Used For

Tempo runs, steady state efforts, aerobic development runs

**McMillan Verified:** Lactate threshold work done at or slightly below threshold (Zone 2) allows more volume without burnout.

## Zone 3 — Lactate Threshold / Tempo

**Purpose:** Lactate threshold improvement, race-pace conditioning

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Target % of Max HR | 80-87% |
| Example (max HR 185) | 148-161 bpm |
| RPE | 6-7 out of 10 |
| Breathing | Hard and rhythmic, requires focus |
| Conversation | A few words only between breaths |
| Feel | Comfortably hard, sustainable 20-40 minutes |
| Pace feel | 10K to half-marathon race pace range |
| Sustainable duration | 20-40 minutes sustained (McMillan verified) |
| Weekly usage | 10-15% of training volume |
| Interval sets | 5-15 minutes at threshold |

### ECHO Focus in Zone 3

- **Economy:** Tests oxygen utilization at threshold — this is where economy improvements become measurable
- **Cadence:** Hold 178-183 SPM under threshold effort
- **Harmony:** Form must hold. Fatigue will try to break posture. *"Shoulders back, head up, arms driving."*
- **Optimization:** Controlled aggression. Not maximum effort. *"This pace should feel controlled-hard, not desperate-hard."*

### Coaching Note on Tempo Intensity (Corrected Data)

Run tempo **mostly at or slightly BELOW lactate threshold**, not right at it. This allows more volume without burnout and produces better long-term adaptation.

## Zone 4 — VO2 Max / Intervals

**Purpose:** VO2 max development, speed endurance, top-end fitness

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Target % of Max HR | 87-95% |
| Example (max HR 185) | 161-176 bpm |
| RPE | 8-9 out of 10 |
| Breathing | Very hard, fast, labored |
| Conversation | Can only count (1, 2, 3) — no real words |
| Feel | Very hard, near-maximum, requires mental effort |
| Pace feel | 3K to 5K race pace range |
| Weekly usage | 8-10% of training volume |
| Interval duration | 2-5 minute intervals |
| Recovery between | 90-120 second easy jogs |
| Frequency | 1× per week maximum |

### VO2 Max Improvement Timeline (Verified)

| Runner Level | Trajectory |
|--------------|-----------|
| Beginner | Fastest gains (2-3 years before plateau) |
| Intermediate | Continued improvement with consistent training |
| Advanced | Maintenance focus, marginal gains require more stimulus |
| 50+ runners | Less frequent VO2 work (1× every 10 days) |

### ECHO Focus in Zone 4

- **Economy:** Maximum oxygen stress. This is the test.
- **Cadence:** Push 185-195 SPM. Explosive leg drive.
- **Harmony:** Form under maximum stress. *"Even when dying, keep arms driving."*
- **Optimization:** Controlled aggression — hard but not wild. Each interval should be consistent, not fading dramatically.

### Example Zone 4 Session

```
Warm-up: 15 minutes easy (Zone 1)

Main set: 5-6 × 3-4 minutes hard (Zone 4)
Recovery: 90-120 seconds easy jog between each

Cool-down: 5-10 minutes easy (Zone 1)
```

**Coach Cue:**
> Push hard, recover smart, repeat. Form quality even when tired — that is the test of real fitness.

## Zone 5 — Maximum / Neuromuscular

**Purpose:** Speed, power, neuromuscular activation, stride mechanics

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Target % of Max HR | 95-100% |
| Example (max HR 185) | 176-185+ bpm |
| RPE | 10 out of 10 |
| Breathing | Maximum effort, gasping |
| Conversation | Impossible |
| Feel | All-out sprint, cannot sustain beyond 20-30 seconds |
| Pace feel | 400m to 1500m race pace — near maximum speed |
| Weekly usage | 2-5% of training volume (strides only) |
| Duration | 15-30 second bursts with full recovery |
| Frequency | 2-3× per week as strides at end of easy runs |

### ECHO Focus in Zone 5

- **Cadence:** 190-200+ SPM target (maximum turnover)
- **Harmony:** Explosive, powerful, full arm drive
- **Economy:** Brief, maximum neuromuscular stimulus
- **Optimization:** Build speed over 20 seconds (0 to 100%), not a flying start

### Strides Protocol

```
4-6 × 20-30 second strides
Build gradually to near-max over 20 seconds
Full recovery jog (90 seconds minimum) between each

When used: After easy runs or day before hard session
```

## Cadence Targets by Zone (Summary)

| Zone | Target SPM | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Zone 1 (Easy) | 170-180 SPM | Light, rhythmic |
| Zone 2 (Steady) | 175-180 SPM | Aerobic, controlled |
| Zone 3 (Tempo) | 178-183 SPM | Threshold, sustained |
| Zone 4 (Intervals) | 185-195 SPM | Hard, explosive |
| Zone 5 (Strides) | 190-200+ SPM | Maximum turnover |

### Key Rule

**Cadence should INCREASE as effort increases.**

A runner with lower cadence at higher effort = overstriding under pressure. This is an **injury risk pattern** — flag it in coaching feedback.

> ℹ️ **EVIDENCE NOTE — INTERNAL CONTRADICTION (per Ian's 2026-07-10 ruling — annotate, don't delete):** This section prescribes absolute per-zone SPM targets, but `ECHO_Framework_CORRECTED.md` Pillar 2 argues cadence is "NOT one-size-fits-all" and that forcing it above a runner's natural rhythm is "counterproductive and injury-prone" — the library contradicts itself, and the evidence sides with the framework. The intervention with trial support is a **5-10% step-rate increase above the runner's own baseline** (Br J Sports Med 2022 step-rate meta-analysis), not an absolute floor; the ubiquitous 180-SPM figure traces to Jack Daniels counting elite runners at the 1984 Olympics — an observation of elites, not a prescription. The tables above stand as typical observed ranges, not as targets to force on any individual runner.

## Weekly Training Intensity Distribution

### Beginner Runners (3 days/week)

| Zone | Percentage | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Zone 1 (Easy) | 80% | Recovery, base |
| Zone 2 (Steady) | 15% | Aerobic development |
| Zone 3-4 (Hard) | 5% | One light tempo or stride session |

### Intermediate Runners (5 days/week)

| Zone | Percentage | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Zone 1 (Easy/Recovery) | 60-65% | Base building |
| Zone 2 (Steady/Tempo) | 20% | Threshold work |
| Zone 3-4 (Intervals/Threshold) | 10-15% | Speed development |
| Zone 5 (Strides) | 5% | Neuromuscular |

### Advanced Runners (6-7 days/week)

| Zone | Percentage | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Zone 1 (Easy/Recovery) | 55-60% | Base maintenance |
| Zone 2 (Steady/Tempo) | 15-20% | Threshold stimulus |
| Zone 3-4 (Intervals/Threshold) | 15-20% | Speed + endurance |
| Zone 5 (Strides/Speed) | 5% | Power development |

## The 80/20 Rule (Verified Principle)

**80% of training should be genuinely easy (Zone 1-2).**  
**20% of training should be hard (Zone 3-5).**

### The Problem

Most recreational runners do the **reverse** — too much moderate effort, not enough truly easy running.

> 📌 **RULING (Ian, 2026-07-10):** This section previously labelled the library's approach "the polarized training model." With ~20% of volume at Zone 3 (threshold) prescribed in the race-specific phase (Phase 3 below), what this library actually prescribes is a **pyramidal** distribution (Seiler; Stöggl & Sperlich 2014) — most volume easy, progressively less at each higher intensity — not polarized, which minimizes the threshold middle. The 80/20 easy-hard emphasis itself is well supported and stands unchanged.

## Resting Heart Rate (RHR) Baselines

**Measurement:** First thing in the morning, before getting up.  
Track for 7 days, calculate average. This is the baseline.

### RHR by Fitness Level

| Level | RHR Range |
|-------|-----------|
| Untrained | 70-80 bpm |
| Recreational runner | 55-70 bpm |
| Intermediate trained | 50-60 bpm |
| Advanced/competitive | 40-55 bpm |
| Elite | Below 40 bpm |

### Resting HR as Recovery Indicator

**If RHR is 5+ bpm above 7-day baseline:**
- Fatigue or early illness
- Action: Replace hard session with easy run or rest

**If RHR is 8-10+ bpm above baseline:**
- Likely illness or significant overtraining
- Action: Rest day mandatory
- Monitor for 2-3 days

### Recovery HR Target (Corrected Data)

**Old metric:** "Declining over weeks" (too vague)

**Correct metric:** Resting HR should be less than 60 bpm for trained runners, or 10-15 bpm above individual resting baseline after full recovery.

## RPE Scale — Rate of Perceived Exertion

| RPE | Zone | Feel | Conversation |
|-----|------|------|--------------|
| 1 | — | Almost no effort. Walking pace. | Easy talk |
| 2 | — | Very light. Easy walk. | Full sentences |
| 3 | Zone 1 | Light. Easy run, fully comfortable. | Full conversation |
| 4 | Z1-Z2 | Moderate. Breathing slightly deeper. | Some effort to speak |
| 5 | Zone 2 | Somewhat hard. Steady state. | Sentences getting shorter |
| 6 | Z2-Z3 | Hard. Tempo effort. | Few words between breaths |
| 7 | Zone 3 | Very hard. Threshold effort. | Near limit of sustainability |
| 8 | Zone 4 | Very very hard. VO2 max effort. | Only counting possible |
| 9 | Z4-Z5 | Near maximum. | Pushing limit |
| 10 | Zone 5 | Maximum effort. All-out sprint. | Impossible to speak |

### Fatigue Detection Using RPE

**If RPE > 8 for two consecutive runs:** Plan softens next session.

**Action:** Replace next hard session with Zone 1 easy run.

⚠️ **This rule is non-negotiable.** Overriding it leads to injury.

## McMillan Periodization Cycles (Verified)

**Macrocycle Length:** 12-24 weeks (goal-dependent)  
**Standard Structure:** 4 phases

### Phase 1 — Build (Weeks 1-6 typical)

**Focus:** Aerobic base, easy volume, form work

| Element | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Intensity distribution | 90% Zone 1, 10% Zone 2 |
| Long run | Building from current base by 10% per week |
| Hard sessions | 0-1 per week (mainly easy volume) |

### Phase 2 — Progressive (Weeks 7-12 typical)

**Focus:** Introducing faster paces, threshold work, hill training

| Element | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Intensity distribution | 70% Zone 1, 20% Zone 2, 10% Zone 3 |
| Long run | Maintaining + introducing moderate finish efforts |
| Hard sessions | 1-2 per week (tempo, hills) |

### Phase 3 — Race-Specific (Weeks 13-18 typical)

**Focus:** Goal-race pace work, race simulations, specificity

| Element | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Intensity distribution | 60% Zone 1, 15% Zone 2, 20% Zone 3, 5% Zone 4 |
| Long run | Race-simulation workouts at goal pace portions |
| Hard sessions | 2-3 per week (goal pace, intervals) |

### Phase 4 — Taper (Final 2 weeks)

**Focus:** Volume reduction, intensity maintained, rest

| Element | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Volume | 50% of peak week |
| Intensity | Same or slightly higher (keep legs sharp) |
| Purpose | Allow adaptation, arrive fresh to race |

> ⚠️ **GAP — NEEDS IAN:** "Final 2 weeks / 50% of peak" is the closest thing to a taper rule in the library, and the worked plans do not follow it consistently: the 5K example tapers 1 week, the 10K ~2 weeks, the marathon ~3 weeks including race week. A generator needs a taper rule keyed to race distance (length, weekly volume percentages, what intensity is retained). See the fuller gap note under General Principles in `ECHO_Training_Plans_McMillan.md`.

### Deload Frequency

**Every 3-4 weeks, reduce volume 35-45%.**

| Setting | Intensity |
|---------|-----------|
| Deload week | 90% Zone 1, 10% Zone 2 |
| Purpose | Non-negotiable for preventing injury and stagnation |
