## Part 1: Running Session Library

---

## Session Type 1: Easy Run (Zone 1)

**Purpose:** Aerobic base building, economy development, active recovery

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 30-60 minutes |
| Frequency | 4-5× per week (majority of all training) |
| Target HR | 60-70% max HR |
| Target cadence | 170-180 SPM |
| RPE | 2-4 out of 10 |

### Structure

1. 5 min very easy warm-up walk/jog
2. Main run at consistent Zone 1 effort
3. 5 min easy cool-down walk
4. Optional: 4-6 × 20 sec strides at end (beginner/intermediate only)

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> This is where economy is built. Keep HR truly low. If you feel like you could go faster, you are at the right pace.

**Cadence:** 
> Target 175 SPM. Light, quick steps. Think floating, not pounding.

**Harmony:** 
> Best run to practice form. Relaxed shoulders, arms at 90 degrees, eyes forward. No fatigue means perfect form practice.

**Optimization:** 
> Discipline run. Resist the urge to race. Patience here builds the engine that makes hard runs possible.

### Common Mistake to Correct

> Your easy run HR was 158 bpm — that is Zone 2, not Zone 1. Slow down by 30-45 seconds per km. It should feel almost too easy. Easy runs done too hard are the number one cause of injury and burnout.

### Fuel

| Duration | Before | After |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Under 45 min | 300 ml water | Normal meal within 2 hours |
| 45-60 min | Light snack 30-60 min before if hungry | Normal meal within 2 hours |

## Session Type 2: Recovery Run (Zone 1 — Very Easy)

**Purpose:** Active recovery, blood flow, psychological maintenance

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 20-30 minutes |
| Frequency | Day after hard sessions |
| Target HR | 55-65% max HR (lower end of Zone 1) |
| Target cadence | 168-175 SPM (light, not forced) |
| RPE | 1-3 out of 10 |

### Structure

- Pure easy running, no structure required
- Stop if any soreness above 3/10
- Walk breaks are fine

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> Zero pace focus. Just move the body.

**Cadence:** 
> Let it be light and natural. Do not force pace.

**Harmony:** 
> This run is about feel-good movement, not performance.

**Optimization:** 
> Active recovery is a training session. Slow is correct.

### Note to Coach AI

Recovery runs should feel embarrassingly easy. If the runner feels good, that is correct. If they feel they are wasting time, that is also correct. That is the point.

### Fuel

None required. Hydrate as normal.

## Session Type 3: Tempo Run (Zone 2-3, Sustained)

**Purpose:** Lactate threshold development, race-pace conditioning

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 35-55 minutes total (including warm-up/cool-down) |
| Frequency | 1× per week |
| Target HR | 75-85% max HR |
| Target cadence | 178-183 SPM |
| RPE | 6-7 out of 10 |

### Structure

1. 10 min easy warm-up (Zone 1)
2. 20-30 min sustained tempo effort (Zone 2-3)
3. 5-10 min easy cool-down (Zone 1)

### ⚠️ Important (Corrected Data)

Tempo runs should be run **at or SLIGHTLY BELOW lactate threshold** — not right at it. This allows more volume without burnout and produces better long-term threshold adaptation.

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> This pace should feel controlled, not desperate. You are stressing the aerobic system efficiently.

**Cadence:** 
> Hold 178-180 SPM even when legs start to feel heavy. Cadence drop here is a signal to ease back slightly.

**Harmony:** 
> Shoulders stay relaxed despite the effort. Strong arm drive. Posture tall — do not hunch as fatigue builds.

**Optimization:** 
> Controlled hard, not maximum hard. There is a clear difference. Controlled = sustainable for 30 minutes. Maximum = you cannot hold it for 30 minutes.

### Pace Guidance

| Level | Guidance |
|-------|----------|
| Beginner | 30-45 sec/km faster than easy pace |
| Intermediate | 45-60 sec/km faster than easy pace |
| Advanced | Race-specific threshold pace |

### Post-Run Feedback Example

> Your tempo pace was consistent throughout (5:28, 5:30, 5:29, 5:31/km). That is excellent execution — controlled and even. Your cadence held at 179 SPM for the first 20 min then dropped to 173 SPM in the final 10 min. Cadence fatigue is the area to improve. Next session: focus on maintaining 178+ SPM even as legs tire.

### Fuel

| Timing | What |
|--------|------|
| 2-3 hours before | Full meal |
| 45 min before | Light snack if needed |
| Post-run | 40-60g carbs + 15-20g protein within 60 minutes |

## Session Type 4: VO2 Max Intervals (Zone 3-4)

**Purpose:** VO2 max development, speed endurance, fitness ceiling raising

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 45-65 minutes total |
| Frequency | 1× per week maximum |
| Target HR | 85-95% max HR during intervals |
| Target cadence | 185-195 SPM during intervals |
| RPE | 8-9 out of 10 during intervals |

### Structure

1. 15 min easy warm-up (Zone 1)
2. 5-6 × 3-4 minutes hard (Zone 3-4) with 90-120 sec easy jog recovery
3. 5-10 min easy cool-down (Zone 1)

### Advanced Structure Options

- 4 × 5 min hard, 2 min recovery
- 6 × 3 min hard, 90 sec recovery
- 8 × 2 min hard, 60 sec recovery (shorter, more reps)

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> Maximum oxygen stress. Each interval tests your aerobic ceiling. Drive efficiency even at top end.

**Cadence:** 
> Push 185-195 SPM. Explosive leg drive. Short, fast strides.

**Harmony:** 
> Form under maximum stress. Drive the arms — they pull the legs forward when legs want to give up.

**Optimization:** 
> Controlled aggression. Hard but not wild. Each interval should be consistent — not blowing up interval 1 and dying in interval 6.

### Key Coaching Rule

First interval and last interval should be within 5-8 seconds of each other per km. If last interval drops more than 10 seconds/km from first: pace was too aggressive. Adjust next session.

### Post-Run Feedback Example

> Intervals: 5:05, 5:08, 5:06, 5:11, 5:18/km. The final interval dropped 13 seconds from your average. This is your current fitness limit. That limit is exactly what this session trains. Next session: start 5 seconds/km slower on interval 1 to allow better consistency across all 5 reps.

### Fuel

| Timing | What |
|--------|------|
| 45 min before | Light snack only (full stomach = GI distress) |
| Post-run | 60-80g carbs + 20-25g protein **WITHIN 30 MINUTES** |

⚠️ **Most critical recovery window of all session types.**

## Session Type 5: Long Run (Zone 1, with Optional Finish Build)

**Purpose:** Aerobic endurance, fat oxidation, mental toughness, economy

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 60-140 minutes (level dependent) |
| Frequency | 1× per week |
| Target HR | 60-70% max HR (Zone 1), can build to Zone 2 final 15-20% |
| Target cadence | 170-178 SPM (start easy, can build) |
| RPE | 3-5 out of 10 (start), 5-6 at finish |

### Duration by Level

| Level | Range |
|-------|-------|
| Beginner | 45-75 min |
| Intermediate | 75-110 min |
| Advanced | 90-140 min (marathon: up to 150 min) |

### Long Run Cap

| Level | Cap |
|-------|-----|
| Beginner | Max 20-25% of weekly km |
| Intermediate | Max 25-30% of weekly km |
| Advanced | Max 30% of weekly km |

### Structure

1. Start at very easy Zone 1 pace (first 20 minutes feel easy)
2. Maintain consistent Zone 1 through the middle
3. Optional: Final 10-20% of run build to Zone 2 (negative split practice)
4. Walk breaks are acceptable for beginners

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> This is the long aerobic investment. Start easy — the adaptation happens when you stay in Zone 1 for duration.

**Cadence:** 
> Even cadence throughout. Watch for SPM dropping in final 20 minutes — that signals fatigue-based form breakdown.

**Harmony:** 
> Mind-body connection is the skill here. Feel your form every 10 minutes. Reset posture, relax shoulders, drive arms.

**Optimization:** 
> Negative split strategy. Save energy early, push the finish. The runner who runs even or negative splits races better than the one who goes out hard and struggles home.

### Fueling During Long Run

- Start fueling at 45 minutes (do not wait until empty)
- Every 45 minutes after that: 30-40g carbs (gel, banana, sports drink)
- Hydration: 500-750 ml per hour depending on temperature
- Hot climate (Thailand): increase to 750-1000 ml per hour

### Post-Run Recovery

60-80g carbs + 20-25g protein **ASAP (within 30 minutes)**

⚠️ **Second most critical recovery window**

## Session Type 6: Fartlek Run (Unstructured Intervals)

**Purpose:** Mental freshness, aerobic and speed development, variety

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 40-55 minutes |
| Frequency | 1× per week (alternative to structured intervals) |
| Target HR | Mix of Zone 1-2 (easy) and Zone 3 (hard) |
| RPE | 3-7 out of 10 (varies) |

### Structure

1. 10 min easy warm-up
2. 20-25 min fartlek main set:
   - Alternate 2-3 min hard (Zone 3) + 1-2 min easy (Zone 1)
   - 4-5 hard/easy cycles
3. 5 min easy cool-down

### Why Fartlek?

Fartlek means "speed play." It removes the pressure of hitting specific pace targets while still building fitness. Excellent for runners who hate structured intervals.

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> Vary your effort. Challenge your aerobic system at different paces.

**Cadence:** 
> Feel the difference between cadence at easy effort and hard effort.

**Harmony:** 
> Hard efforts teach you to hold form under fatigue.

**Optimization:** 
> Push hard when it feels right. Recover when you need it. This is adaptable training.

### Fuel

| Timing | What |
|--------|------|
| 1-2 hours before | Light meal |
| Post-run | 40-60g carbs + 15-20g protein within 60 minutes |

## Session Type 7: Threshold / Cruise Intervals

**Purpose:** Lactate threshold building, sustained hard effort practice

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 50-70 minutes total |
| Frequency | 1× per week (alternative to tempo runs) |
| Target HR | 80-90% max HR |
| RPE | 7-8 out of 10 |

### Structure

1. 15 min easy warm-up
2. 3-4 × 2-3 km at Zone 2-3 effort (threshold), 1-2 min easy recovery between
3. 10 min easy cool-down

### How Cruise Intervals Differ from Tempo

**Tempo:** One long 20-30 min sustained effort  
**Cruise intervals:** Multiple 2-3 km repeats with brief recovery

Both build threshold, but cruise intervals allow more volume at hard effort.

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> Threshold pace is sustainable hard. Not maximum hard.

**Cadence:** 
> Maintain 180+ SPM throughout all repeats.

**Harmony:** 
> Hold form on each repeat — they should look the same.

**Optimization:** 
> Each repeat should be consistent in pace (within 5 seconds/km).

### Fuel

Same as Tempo Run (see above)

## Session Type 8: Hill Repeats (Power / Strength)

**Purpose:** Leg strength, power development, running economy

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 40-55 minutes total |
| Frequency | 1-2× per week (substitute for flat intervals) |
| Target grade | 3-5% gradient (gentle, sustained uphill) |
| Duration per repeat | 3-4 minutes hard uphill |
| Frequency per session | 6-8 repeats |

### Structure

1. 10 min easy warm-up
2. 6-8 × 3 min hard uphill (Zone 3-4), jog/walk down recovery
3. 10 min easy cool-down

### Why Hills?

Hills naturally increase cadence, strengthen glutes, and build power without high speed. Safer than flat interval work for injury prevention.

### ECHO Cues

**Economy:** 
> Push hard uphill. Hills build the strength that makes flat ground fast.

**Cadence:** 
> Shorter strides uphill — maintain rhythm even as gradient increases.

**Harmony:** 
> Slight forward lean into the hill. Drive arms to pull legs forward.

**Optimization:** 
> Controlled effort uphill. Recovery jog down — easy and long to protect knees.

### Fuel

Same as VO2 Max Intervals (see above)

## Session Type 9: Strides (Neuromuscular Activation)

**Purpose:** Speed, power, neuromuscular activation, stride mechanics

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | 20-30 seconds × 4-6 reps |
| Frequency | 2-3× per week (end of easy runs) |
| Target HR | 95-100% max HR (max effort) |
| RPE | 9-10 out of 10 |

### Structure

1. After an easy run, do 4-6 strides:
   - 20-30 second build-up from easy to near-max speed
   - Full recovery jog (90 seconds minimum) between each
   - Not flying starts — gradual acceleration

### Why Strides Matter

Strides maintain neuromuscular activation and speed without the fatigue of full interval workouts. Non-negotiable for maintaining speediness.

### ECHO Cues

**Cadence:** 
> 190-200+ SPM. Feel fast leg turnover.

**Harmony:** 
> Explosive, powerful, full arm drive. Push through the finish.

**Optimization:** 
> Build speed gradually — 0% to 100% over 20 seconds, not flying starts.

### When to Use

- End of easy runs (2-3× per week)
- Day before hard session (1× per week)
- During deload weeks (maintain feel for speed)
- Never after hard sessions

## Part 2: Cross-Training Library

## Cross-Training: Swimming (Aerobic / Recovery)

**Zone equivalent:** Zone 1-2 depending on intensity  
**Duration:** 30-45 minutes  
**Frequency:** 1-2× per week  
**Used for:** Active recovery, aerobic capacity building without impact

### Volume Equivalence

Counts as **50% of equivalent running distance**

Example: 60 min swimming = equivalent of ~30 min running for load

### ECHO Integration

**Economy:** 
> Smooth, rhythmic strokes. Focus on efficiency over power.

**Harmony:** 
> Smooth hip rotation. Streamlined body position.

**Optimization:** 
> Maintain steady pace and rhythm. Build consistency.

### Best For

Injured runners, high-mileage weeks, active recovery, non-impact aerobic work

### Caution

Chlorine can dry out skin; ensure hydration before and after.

---

## Cross-Training: Cycling (Aerobic / Stamina)

**Zone equivalent:** Zone 1-2 (steady), can go Zone 2-3 (tempo)  
**Duration:** 45-90 minutes  
**Frequency:** 1× per week  
**Used for:** Building aerobic base, unloading impact stress, building bike legs

### Volume Equivalence

Counts as **60% of equivalent running distance**

Example: 60 min cycling = equivalent of ~36 min running for load

### ECHO Integration

**Economy:** 
> Smooth cadence at 80-90 RPM. Efficiency over power.

**Cadence:** 
> Spin not grind. High cadence cycling = better running cadence feel.

**Harmony:** 
> Upright posture, relaxed grip, steady rhythm.

**Optimization:** 
> Zone 1-2 cycling is active recovery. Resist pushing hard.

### Best For

Building endurance without pounding, active recovery after heavy training weeks

### Caution

Monitor knee position (knee should track over toe, not cave in).  
Avoid excessive resistance — protect knee joints.

---

## Cross-Training: Elliptical (Aerobic / Recovery)

**Zone equivalent:** Zone 1-2  
**Duration:** 30-40 minutes  
**Frequency:** 1× per week  
**Used for:** Zero-impact aerobic work, recovery weeks, joint rest

### Volume Equivalence

Counts as **40% of equivalent running distance**  
(lower impact = lower adaptation stimulus)

### ECHO Integration

**Cadence:** 
> Target 160+ steps per minute — mimics running cadence without impact.

**Harmony:** 
> Smooth, gliding motion. No bouncing or jerking.

**Optimization:** 
> Light resistance. This is recovery work, not fitness building.

### Best For

Very tired or sore runners, deload weeks, runners with joint pain that limits running

### Limitation

Does not build running-specific strength or neuromuscular patterns as effectively as actual running. Supplement only, not replacement.

---

## Cross-Training: Rowing (Aerobic + Strength)

**Zone equivalent:** Zone 1-2 (steady), Zone 3 (hard efforts)  
**Duration:** 30-45 minutes  
**Frequency:** 1× per week  
**Used for:** Full-body conditioning, upper body strength, aerobic work

### Volume Equivalence

Counts as **70% of equivalent running distance**  
PLUS additional upper body and core strength credit

### ECHO Integration

**Economy:** 
> Smooth catch-drive-finish cycle. Power efficiency.

**Harmony:** 
> Legs initiate, core transfers, arms finish. Full chain.

**Optimization:** 
> Steady power output. Consistent split times.

### Best For

Strength-building cross-training, intermediate to advanced runners wanting full-body aerobic work

### Caution

High lower back load — ensure proper rowing form.  
Not recommended for runners with existing lower back issues.

---

## Cross-Training: Stair Climbing (Stamina / Leg Strength)

**Zone equivalent:** Zone 2-3 (typically hard effort)  
**Duration:** 20-30 minutes  
**Frequency:** 1× every 10 days  
**Used for:** Hill training substitute, leg power development

### Volume Equivalence

Counts as **60% of equivalent running distance**  
PLUS lower body strength credit

### ECHO Integration

**Economy:** 
> Controlled pace. Push through midfoot on each step.

**Cadence:** 
> Maintain consistent rhythm up the stairs.

**Harmony:** 
> Upright posture, slight forward lean, strong arm drive.

**Optimization:** 
> Sustainable pace — not a sprint.

### Best For

Runners without access to hills, power development, building stamina in legs

### Caution

Very demanding. Use sparingly to avoid overtraining.  
Do NOT use day before or after hard running sessions.

---

## Cross-Training: Strength Circuit (Prehab + Power)

**Zone equivalent:** Zone 4 (neuromuscular — not aerobic)  
**Duration:** 30-45 minutes  
**Frequency:** 2× per week  
**Used for:** Injury prevention, leg power, running economy support

### Volume Equivalence

**0% running distance equivalent**  
Contributes to strength development and injury resilience only

### Beginner Structure Example

1. Glute bridges: 3 × 12
2. Lateral lunges: 3 × 8 each side
3. Single-leg RDL: 3 × 8 each side
4. Calf raises (eccentric): 3 × 12 each leg
5. Plank: 3 × 30 seconds
6. Lateral band walks: 3 × 15 each direction

### Intermediate/Advanced Structure Example

1. Single-leg glute bridge: 3 × 15 each
2. Bulgarian split squat: 3 × 10 each leg
3. Single-leg RDL with dumbbell: 3 × 10 each
4. Eccentric calf raises off step: 3 × 15 each
5. Side plank: 3 × 45 sec each side
6. Lateral band walks: 3 × 20 each direction

### ECHO Integration

**Harmony:** 
> Controlled movements. Form quality over speed or weight.

**Economy:** 
> Stronger glutes = better running economy and hip stability.

**Optimization:** 
> Consistent reps, controlled tempo. Not rushing.

### Best For

All runners. Especially important in Build phase.  
Non-negotiable for runners 50+.

### Schedule

On easy run days only (after run, not before).  
Never on day before hard running sessions.

## Part 3: Deload Week Architecture

### Definition

A deload week is a **planned reduction in training stress** (volume, intensity, or both) to allow adaptation, recovery, and accumulation of training benefits. Without deload, runners stagnate or get injured.

**Standard Protocol:** Every 3-4 weeks, reduce volume by 20-30%.

---

### Beginner Deload (3-day plan)

**Normal week:** ~2.5 hours running  
**Deload week:** ~1.5 hours running (40% reduction)  
**Intensity:** Zone 1 only. No hard efforts.

| Day | Activity | Duration |
|-----|----------|----------|
| Monday | 20 min easy | 20 min |
| Wednesday | 25 min easy | 25 min |
| Saturday | 30 min easy long run (very relaxed, not proper long run) | 30 min |

---

### Intermediate Deload (5-day plan)

**Normal week structure:**

| Day | Activity | Duration | Zone |
|-----|----------|----------|------|
| Monday | Easy run | 40 min | Z1 |
| Tuesday | Tempo | 35 min | Z2 |
| Wednesday | Easy run | 45 min | Z1 |
| Thursday | Intervals | 45 min | Z3 |
| Friday | Rest + prehab | — | — |
| Saturday | Long run | 90 min | Z1 |
| Sunday | Rest | — | — |

**Total:** ~5 hours, 60% Z1, 20% Z2, 20% Z3

**Deload week structure:**

| Day | Activity | Duration | Zone |
|-----|----------|----------|------|
| Monday | Easy run | 25 min | Z1 |
| Tuesday | Easy + Zone 2 | 30 min total | Z1-Z2 |
| Wednesday | Easy run | 30 min | Z1 |
| Thursday | Easy run | 25 min | Z1 |
| Friday | Rest + easy mobility | — | — |
| Saturday | Easy run (relaxed) | 45 min | Z1 |
| Sunday | Rest + recovery focus | — | — |

**Total:** ~2.75 hours (45% reduction), 90% Z1, 10% Z2, 0% Z3

---

### Advanced Deload (6-7 day plan)

**Normal week:** 65-75 km  
**Deload week:** 40-45 km (35-40% reduction)  
**Hard sessions:** None. Zero.  
**Structure:** Easy running only, with 2-3 strides at end of runs to maintain neuromuscular activation

---

### ECHO Cues for All Deload Weeks

**Economy:** 
> Very relaxed effort. Focus on smooth movement, not pace.

**Cadence:** 
> Slightly higher than normal — 180+ SPM to keep legs fresh and light.

**Harmony:** 
> Perfect opportunity to work on form with no fatigue interference.

**Optimization:** 
> Cruise. Do not push. Let your body absorb all the training stress from the past 3 weeks.

---

### What NOT to Do During Deload

❌ Switch to hill repeats or speed work (defeats purpose)  
❌ Add extra gym sessions (adds stress when reducing running)  
❌ Try a new race distance or test fitness (disrupts recovery)  
❌ Ignore the deload and train normally (accumulates fatigue)  
❌ Feel guilty about reduced volume (this is part of the plan)

---

### Expected Deload Outcomes

| Timeline | Outcome |
|----------|---------|
| Day 1-2 | May feel sluggish (normal — body is absorbing training) |
| Day 3-4 | Energy and enthusiasm return |
| Day 5-6 | Slight performance improvement on easy efforts |
| Week after | Breakthrough feeling — harder workouts feel more manageable |

---

### Deload Frequency by Runner Type

| Type | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Speedsters (more speed work, higher intensity) | Every 3 weeks |
| Endurance athletes (higher volume, lower intensity) | Every 4 weeks |
| Low-volume beginners (under 30 km/week) | Every 4-5 weeks |
| Runners 50+ | Every 3 weeks (mandatory — slower recovery) |
| High stress life periods | Every 3 weeks regardless of level |

---

### Deload Runner-Type Adaptations

**Speedsters:** 
Keep 4 × 20 sec strides during deload to maintain neuromuscular connection. No threshold or interval work.

**Endurance athletes:** 
One slightly longer easy run (45-50 min max). Extra focus on movement quality and form.

**Combo runners:** 
Mix of above — light steady effort (20 min Zone 2) once, plus movement quality focus.
