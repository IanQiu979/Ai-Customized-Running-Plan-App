# Market research: how real 5K plans size tempo and interval sessions

**Question being validated:** the app's generated 5K plan drew a 3/10 from its own McMillan-certified
coach-owner, with one named defect — tempo/interval sessions scale up with weekly volume, producing
things like a 14 km tempo session inside a 5K plan. This report checks that defect against what
actually-published, reputable 5K plans and methodologies do, so the fix is grounded in real coaching
practice rather than invented.

Sources consulted directly (fetched and read) or via search snippets are cited inline per claim. Where
a number could not be confirmed against a primary source (e.g., Pfitzinger's actual 5K-plan tables,
which are paywalled inside the book and not reproduced online), it is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

---

## 1. Tempo sizing

**Finding: tempo/threshold work is bounded by a fixed physiological duration, not scaled arithmetically
with weekly volume — and 5K-plan tempos are dramatically smaller than marathon-plan tempos.**

- **Hal Higdon (Intermediate & Advanced 5K)** — [Intermediate 5K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/intermediate-5k/), [Advanced 5K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/advanced-5k/): the table entry is a duration, not a distance — "30 min tempo" progressing to 35, 40, then back to 30 (Intermediate, 8 weeks) or 30→45→30 (Advanced, 8 weeks). Higdon defines his tempo run as **one continuous progressive run**: "5-10 minutes easy running, continue with 10-15 faster running [near 10K pace], and finish with 5-10 minutes cooling down." That means the 30-45 minute headline figure is the **whole session**, but the actual sustained-quality portion embedded in it is only **10-15 minutes** — smaller than the label suggests. Progression is driven by week-in-plan, not by the runner's weekly mileage (which itself barely moves, 3-3.5 easy-run miles most weeks).
- **Jack Daniels' Running Formula** — T-pace ("threshold") is explicitly defined as *the fastest pace you can hold for 60 minutes non-stop* — a hard physiological ceiling. Continuous T-pace tempo runs are prescribed at **20-60 minutes**, with a volume rule that **no more than 10% of weekly mileage** should be run at T pace in a single session ([runningtimecalculator.com](https://runningtimecalculator.com/en/training-paces.html), [marktosques.com](https://marktosques.com/running/jack-daniels-phase-ii/), [physicalrules.com PDF](https://physicalrules.com/edslessons.com/ThresholdtrainingJD.pdf)). Note the two caps work together: the 10%-of-mileage rule and the "sustainable for 60 min" pace definition mean a single T-pace session **cannot balloon past roughly 60 minutes / ~10-12 km even for a very high-mileage runner** — it is capped, not proportional.
- **Pete Pfitzinger, "Faster Road Racing: 5K to Half Marathon"** — LT/tempo pace is defined as 20-30 sec/mile slower than 5K race pace (10-15 sec/mile slower than 10K pace). "Classic Tempo Runs" generally run 20-40 minutes continuous at LT pace. The book's actual 5K-plan week-by-week distance table was **not accessible online** (paywalled/print-only) — mark the exact 5K-plan session distance **UNVERIFIED**. What is verifiable, and directly useful as a contrast: Pfitzinger's **marathon-plan** LT sessions (same author, same LT-pace concept, different race distance) run **5-6 miles (8-10 km) continuous at LT pace**, inside a session that totals **8-9 miles** with warm-up/cool-down — roughly triple the continuous-effort volume of what every other 5K-focused source in this report prescribes. This is the clearest evidence of race-distance-dependence: the same coach, same LT concept, scales the number up ~3x only when the target race itself is ~8x longer.
- **McMillan Running** — [5K Training Plan Guide](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/5k-training-plan-guide/): tempo runs are "a continuous run lasting **15-40 minutes**" at lactate-threshold pace — again a fixed band, not mileage-scaled.
- **RunnersConnect**, [Workouts for the 5K](https://runnersconnect.net/workouts-for-the-5k/): 5K-specific tempo = **12-20 minutes continuous** at LT pace (up to 30 min for advanced runners), wrapped in a **10 min warm-up + 5-10 min cool-down**, at a pace ~25-30 sec/mile slower than 5K goal pace. Full session ≈ 30-45 min / roughly 5-7 km depending on pace.
- **Runna** — [Ultimate 5K Training Guide](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/6636429-the-ultimate-5k-training-guide): tempo = continuous effort at 70-80% effort with no set rest periods; the app's 80:20 rule caps time at-or-above threshold to 20% of total training time, again a percentage cap rather than 1:1 scaling with weekly volume.
- Cross-check on the marathon-vs-5K contrast from general sources: 5K/10K tempos are typically cited at **15-30 minutes**, with marathon tempos or tempo-finish long runs cited at **45-60 minutes or 6-10 miles**, e.g. a 28 km marathon long run with a 10-12 km tempo segment embedded in it (secondary sources, e.g. Canadian Running Magazine, Runstreet — general guidance, not a specific plan).

**Bottom line:** across every source that addresses 5K training specifically, sustained tempo/threshold
*work* clusters at **roughly 15-30 minutes continuous**, full session (with warm-up/cool-down) at
**roughly 25-45 minutes / 4-7 km**. Nothing in this survey — including the marathon-plan comparators —
supports a 14 km tempo session for a 5K plan at any weekly-volume level. Even Daniels' most generous
percentage-of-mileage rule, applied to a very high weekly volume, is bounded by the 60-minutes-sustainable
ceiling long before it reaches 14 km.

## 2. Interval prescriptions

**Finding: total quality volume for a 5K-specific interval session converges tightly around 3-5 km
across sources, almost regardless of which rep distance is chosen.**

### Rep distance menu
200m (Higdon Advanced buildup weeks), 400m (Higdon both plans, McMillan buildup, RunnersConnect, Nike
Run Club, mymottiv 12-week plan), 600m (McMillan buildup), 800m (McMillan buildup, RunnersConnect),
1000m (McMillan's flagship "Best 5K Workout," Daniels I-pace, RunnersConnect), 1200m (Daniels Phase III),
mile/1600m (Daniels cruise-interval T-pace reps, not typically I-pace for 5K prep).

### Total quality volume per session (excluding WU/CD) — the convergent number
McMillan's own flagship workout page, [The Best 5K Workout](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/best-5k-workout/),
gives four interchangeable session designs, and they land in a tight band regardless of rep length:

| Session | Reps × distance | Recovery | Total quality volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flagship (9-12 days pre-race) | 5 × 1000m | 400-600m jog | **5.0 km** |
| Buildup A | 10-12 × 400m | 200-300m jog | **4.0-4.8 km** |
| Buildup B | 8 × 600m | 300-400m jog | **4.8 km** |
| Buildup C | 6 × 800m | 400-500m jog | **4.8 km** |

All anchored to **"goal 5K pace."** Higdon's more novice-oriented plans sit at the lower end: 5-8 × 400m
= **2.0-3.2 km** ([Intermediate](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/intermediate-5k/),
[Advanced](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/advanced-5k/)). Aggregated
secondary guidance (run247.com, training4endurance.co.uk) converges on the same shape: **"8% of weekly
volume, up to a maximum of ~10 km"** — i.e., a percentage cap with an absolute ceiling, structurally
identical to Daniels' rule for T-pace (see §1), which independently states **I-pace ≤ 8% of weekly
mileage, R-pace ≤ 5%** (secondary synthesis via sport-calculator.com, vdoto2.com — Daniels' book itself
not directly fetched).

### Pace anchor — a genuine methodology split
Two real, disagreeing conventions exist:
- **Goal/target race pace** — McMillan's flagship session, Higdon's 400m reps, Runna's example ("run at
  your target 5k pace for 400m") all anchor reps to the runner's **goal** 5K pace. This is the practical,
  popular-app convention.
- **Current fitness (VDOT), never goal pace** — Daniels is explicit and emphatic that using a goal-time
  VDOT instead of a current one is "the cardinal sin... the express route to overtraining and injury."
  I-pace and T-pace are derived from a **recent actual performance**, not an aspiration.
  ([coacheseducation.com](https://www.coacheseducation.com/endur/jack-daniels-nov-00.php), secondary
  synthesis of the VDOT tables.)

This is a real split worth naming rather than collapsing: consumer plans (Higdon, McMillan's public
articles, Runna) key off goal pace; the more rigorous VDOT method keys off current fitness. Which one a
generator uses should be a stated design decision, not an accident.

### Progression across a 10-12 week plan
Two consistent patterns across sources:
- **Higdon** (both plans): rep *count* rises across weeks (5→6→7→8 for 400s, or 8→9→10 for 200s) while
  rep distance and pace stay constant — volume grows by adding reps, not by lengthening or accelerating
  them.
- **Daniels' phase structure** (via [runningwithrock.com review](https://runningwithrock.com/review-jack-daniels-5k-10k-training-plan/)):
  Phase II emphasizes **Repetition** work (200-400m, faster than race pace, long recoveries) early;
  Phase III shifts to **Interval** work (800-1200m, closer to 5K/10K race effort) later — i.e. reps
  *do* get longer and pace shifts toward race-specificity as the race approaches.
- A secondary 12-week breakdown ([mymottiv.com](https://www.mymottiv.com/5k-training-plan/12-week-intermediate-5k-training-plan)) shows the same arc: hill reps early → short 400m reps at
  5K-pace-minus mid-plan → longer 200-350m reps at goal pace late, with a final taper.

**Consensus shape:** early season = shorter reps, faster-than-race pace, longer recovery (builds
neuromuscular speed and economy); late season = reps closer to race-specific pace, sometimes longer,
tighter recovery (race-specificity); final 1-2 weeks = volume cut sharply while intensity is held
(taper). Nothing in any source has reps or session volume growing in step with the runner's *weekly
mileage tier* — they grow in step with *proximity to race day*.

### Recovery between reps
Predominantly **jog**, sized relative to rep distance/time rather than fixed:
- McMillan: recovery jog ≈ 40-67% of the rep distance (400-600m jog after 1000m reps; 200-300m after
  400m; 300-400m after 600m; 400-500m after 800m).
- RunnersConnect: 90-120 sec jog after 400m reps.
- Runna: 60-90 sec **walking** rest after 400m reps at 5K pace.
- Higdon: jog and/or walk equal to the rep distance (400m jog after 400m hard).
- General VO2max-interval guideline (via run247/RunnersConnect aggregation): recovery jog = 50-90% of
  rep *time*, trending toward 50-75% once reps reach 4 minutes or longer.

## 3. Notation & abbreviations

- **Daniels' Running Formula**: the canonical five-letter system — **E**asy, **M**arathon, **T**hreshold,
  **I**nterval, **R**epetition — each tied to a %VDOT band. This is the most widely cited notation system
  in the sport and the one most other systems get compared against.
- **Hal Higdon**: no abbreviation system at all — plain-English table cells ("5 x 400", "30 min tempo",
  "4 mi fast", "Rest"). Notable because it's one of the most-followed plans on the internet and
  deliberately avoids jargon.
- **Runna**: avoids letter codes entirely in favor of spelled-out session names (Interval Session, Tempo
  Run, Recovery Run, Long Run, Time Trial, Strides) plus a numeric **RPE 1-10** effort scale, and spells
  out pace anchors ("target 5k pace") rather than abbreviating them.
- **Nike Run Club**: spells out pace targets in guided-run scripts ("5K Pace," "Mile Pace," "10K Pace")
  with an itemized "5:00 Warm Up" line, e.g. an interval set written as "8 x 1:00 @ 5K Pace, 1:00
  recovery between all intervals" ([Nike 5K Training Plan PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-5K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf)).
- **Informal coach/community shorthand** (representative reference: [Wrinkled Runner, "Workout
  Shorthand"](https://wrinkledrunner.com/workout-shorthand/)): **WU** = warm-up, **CD** = cool-down,
  **ez** = easy, **rec** = recovery, **@[distance] pace** (e.g. "@5k", "@goal pace") = pace anchor,
  `count × distance/time` = rep prescription. Example compact notation given on that page: *"2 mi
  ez/8 x 100m @5k with 200m rec/1 mi CD"*. This WU/CD/ez/rec/@pace convention is the closest thing to a
  de facto standard across independently published coach plans and spreadsheets, though it is **not**
  a single official spec — I could not verify a formal "GP / ER / TR" two-letter legend as an
  official TrainingPeaks convention; mark that specific claim **UNVERIFIED**. TrainingPeaks' own help
  docs confirm only WU and CD as standard terms ([TrainingPeaks Glossary](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001271712-Glossary)).

### Do apps ship a glossary screen?
Yes — Runna publishes a dedicated public glossary, [Coaching Terms Every Runner Should Know](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/9459885-coaching-terms-every-runner-should-know),
covering: Cadence, Conversational Pace, Cool Down, Cross-training, Deload, Easy Run, Hill Repeats,
Interval Session, Lactate Threshold, Long Run, Recovery Run, RPE, Steady Pace, Strides, Tapering, Tempo
Session, Threshold Run, Time Trial, Warm Up. Each is a **full term + one-line plain-English definition**
— notably *not* a dense letter-code legend. This suggests the more consumer-friendly convention for a
glossary screen is spelled-out terms with short definitions, RPE anchoring, and pace-name callouts,
rather than a Daniels-style letter system, which is aimed at more technical/veteran users.

## 4. Strides

- **Runna** (glossary definition): "short, fast bursts of running (**10-20 seconds**)" at **85-90% max
  effort**, described as boosting turnover and form; framed as something appended after runs.
- **RunnersConnect's 5K warm-up protocol** specifically (distinct use case — priming immediately before a
  hard session rather than a standalone easy-day add-on): "run 2-3 short strides (**80% effort, 100m
  each**) with full recovery" as part of the pre-quality-session warm-up.
- **Aggregated general coaching convention** (RunnersConnect "How to Do Strides," coachsaltmarsh.com,
  marathonhandbook.com — secondary sources, broadly consistent with each other): **70-100m** or
  **~20-30 second** accelerations at **~90% effort**; build gradually over the first 25-30m, hold, then
  float; **4-8 reps per session**, **2-3x per week**; full recovery (60-90s walk/jog) between reps;
  placed at the **end of an easy run**, never before a hard/quality session (except the distinct
  pre-workout-primer use case above) and never as a standalone workout.
- **Higdon**: notably **absent** as a standing weekly prescription in the Novice, Intermediate, or
  Advanced 5K plans reviewed. His only mention of short fast bursts is "a few sprints of 100 meters" as
  part of the interval-day warm-up routine — not a recurring easy-day stride set. Worth flagging as a
  real gap between the most-followed free plan and the more current McMillan/Runna/RunnersConnect
  convention, which all treat strides as standard weekly maintenance.

## 5. Warm-up/cool-down accounting

**Finding: the dominant convention is that the headline quality number is work-only; WU/CD are itemized
separately and additively, not folded into the headline figure.** Higdon's tempo-run label is the one
partial exception.

- **McMillan** "Best 5K Workout": the 5×1000m / 10-12×400m / etc. figures are explicitly the rep
  work only; "a thorough warm-up" is mentioned as a separate prerequisite, not counted in the total.
- **Daniels / Pfitzinger-style LT runs**: described as e.g. "5-6 miles at LT pace" with a **20-minute
  warm-up** and **10-15 minute cool-down** itemized as distinct, separately-stated segments around the
  work.
- **Nike Run Club**: itemizes "**5:00 Warm Up**" as its own line before the interval set; the interval
  prescription itself ("8 x 1:00 @ 5K Pace") is work-only.
- **RunnersConnect** tempo breakdown: "10 min easy, then 12-20 min tempo pace, then 5-10 min cool down"
  — all three segments stated separately; the "12-20 min" is the number that gets called "the tempo," not
  the 30-40 min full-session total.
- **Higdon — the exception**: his tempo-run entries ("30 min tempo," "40 min tempo") are the **full
  continuous session** (easy-start + surge + ease-down all in one run), not the sustained-quality portion
  alone. His *interval*-day entries ("5 x 400") are work-only, with warm-up/cool-down separately
  instructed in prose and excluded from both the table cell and the weekly mileage arithmetic.

**Bottom line:** work-only headline numbers are the majority convention (McMillan, Daniels, Pfitzinger's
LT-run description, Nike, RunnersConnect, and Higdon's own interval days). Higdon's tempo-day labeling is
the one place in this survey where "tempo minutes" means the whole session rather than the sustained
effort — worth naming explicitly as a trap, since a generator that copies Higdon's tempo convention while
intending McMillan/Daniels-style "quality volume" semantics is exactly the kind of category confusion
that could produce an oversized number.

## Implications for our generator

1. **The core defect (tempo scaling with weekly volume) has no support anywhere in this survey.** Every
   method either fixes sustained tempo duration to a physiological band (15-40 min) regardless of weekly
   mileage, or caps it as a *small percentage* of weekly mileage with a hard absolute ceiling (Daniels:
   ≤10% of weekly miles, and never past ~60 min / ~10-12 km even at high volume). A 14 km tempo in a 5K
   plan matches nothing here — it's roughly what you'd only see embedded in a **marathon**-plan long run
   with a tempo finish, per Pfitzinger's marathon-plan comparator (5-6 mi / 8-10 km continuous LT, inside
   an 8-9 mi session).

2. **Size quality sessions off race distance first, weekly volume second.** Recommend hard-coding a
   5K-plan band: tempo sustained work 15-30 min (full session 25-40 min including WU/CD), interval
   session quality volume 3-5 km (ceiling ~8-10 km only for the highest-volume advanced tier, via a
   capped percentage rule, never linear scaling). This mirrors McMillan's four interchangeable
   session designs, which land in the same 4-5 km band whether reps are 400m or 1000m — a good check
   for the plan-shape spec's rep-distance × rep-count table.

3. **Make the WU/CD convention explicit in the plan-shape spec and stick to work-only headline
   numbers** (McMillan/Daniels/Nike/RunnersConnect convention), itemizing warm-up and cool-down as
   separate segments. Higdon's tempo-day labeling — where "30 min tempo" is a full continuous session,
   not the sustained-quality portion — is a plausible source of exactly this kind of confusion if the
   generator absorbed that convention while intending McMillan/Daniels-style "quality volume" semantics
   elsewhere in the same plan.

4. **Decide and document the pace anchor.** McMillan, Higdon, and Runna anchor 5K reps to *goal* race
   pace; Daniels anchors to *current*-fitness VDOT and treats goal-pace anchoring as a named mistake.
   Given the project already estimates paces from a recent time (per the intake fields), a
   Daniels-style current-fitness anchor is the more defensible default for safety — but this should be
   an explicit ruling, not an implicit accident, since real coaches split on it.

5. **Add an explicit progression rule**: early-plan reps shorter/faster-than-race pace with more
   recovery; late-plan reps closer to goal pace (optionally longer), tighter recovery; final 1-2 weeks
   cut volume while holding intensity. None of the surveyed sources scale session size by the runner's
   *weekly-volume tier* — they scale by *proximity to race day*.

6. **Consider adding strides** (4-8 × ~20-30 sec / 70-100m, ~85-90% effort, end of easy days, 2-3x/week)
   as standard weekly maintenance — McMillan, Runna, and RunnersConnect all include them; Higdon's
   public plans are the outlier in omitting them, and that omission isn't a strong precedent to follow
   given it leaves the plan with no low-risk speed stimulus outside the two quality days.
